# What the survey says is missing

Eight gaps that recur across families — each a place where a new framework could claim
territory rather than incremental improvement.

## 1 · Capacity is almost never separated from preference

Nearly every model represents reluctance to reduce contacts as a *preference* parameter.
The empirical literature says the binding constraint is usually the *inability* to reduce
them. Only the superspreader paper (*J. R. Soc. Interface* 2023) and the mobility-network
work (*Nature* 2021) treat constraint as its own object. In a prescribed-rule model the
distinction is invisible, because "won't" and "can't" generate identical trajectories.

## 2 · Edge decisions are unilateral

Across the rewiring and dropping families, one endpoint acts and the other is passive.
Contacts in reality require mutual consent to exist and unilateral action to end. No
model in this survey implements that asymmetry.

## 3 · Behavioral preferences are static

Fatigue has arrived (2026), but as an aggregate adherence parameter. No model in this
survey carries risk tolerance as a per-node state variable evolving from that node's own
history — though Manrubia & Zanette come closest by letting *selection*, rather than
adaptation, reshape the population's risk profile across waves.

## 4 · Perfect detection is nearly universal

Almost every avoidance model assumes susceptibles know which neighbours are infectious.
Chai & Karaliopoulos (2026) show this matters enormously — link-breaking is far less
effective when infection is undetectable — and Espinoza et al. (2021) show the same in
mean-field. Network models with derived decisions under imperfect detection do not exist
yet.

## 5 · Higher-order adaptation is entirely rule-based

Three 2025–2026 papers add adaptation to hypergraph contagion, all with prescribed rules.
Given that hyperedge overlap is what drives explosive transitions, whether a
utility-maximising agent preferentially avoids high-overlap groups is both open and
physically consequential.

## 6 · Behavior and its determinants are conflated

The 2025 socio-epidemic review tested this and found behavioral responses poorly
explained by awareness, beliefs or trust. A large fraction of the awareness-contagion
literature assumes precisely that correlation.

## 7 · The network after the epidemic is never examined

Every adaptive model either restores edges completely or conserves degree through
rewiring. The panel evidence says ties are permanently lost and networks restructure
toward kin. No epidemic network model produces a post-epidemic network as an output.

## 8 · Identifiability is a known and unsolved problem

Two of the most cited recent papers in the field are about the difficulty of fitting
behavioral parameters at all. Any new mechanism adds parameters to a model class that
already struggles to justify the ones it has — which argues for mechanisms producing
**qualitatively distinct, observable signatures** rather than better fits.

---

## The positioning in one sentence

The utility-maximisation generator is well established in mean-field and almost absent on
networks; the temporary-deletion action is well established on networks and always driven
by an assigned rate. This framework already occupies the intersection. A new framework's
claim to novelty is strongest if it extends along the axes the whole field has left
static — **what the agent can see, what constrains it, and how its preferences change** —
rather than along the substrate axis, where the competition is dense and fast-moving.
