# Literature survey: behavior on networks

A survey of how human behavior has been coupled to epidemic spread on networks,
compiled September 2026. Roughly 100 works spanning 1998–2026.

- [`taxonomy.md`](taxonomy.md) — the four-axis classification and the positioning grid
- [`compilation.md`](compilation.md) — the works, grouped into fourteen families
- [`gaps.md`](gaps.md) — eight gaps the survey reveals

A browsable version of the same material, with the taxonomy tables and the
action-by-generator grid rendered, is maintained separately:
**[The Behavior-Network Atlas](https://claude.ai/artifact/WNM1t8P6HaET2bYV7LfJbJ)**.

## Scope

The survey covers models in which behavior and epidemic dynamics are coupled on an
explicit contact structure, plus the mean-field antecedents of each family where those
are the origin of the approach. It deliberately includes phenomenological, rewiring,
awareness-contagion, game-theoretic, utility-based, and learned-policy approaches,
since the point is to position a new framework against the whole field rather than
against one part of it.

## The organizing claim

Papers are usually described by their **substrate** — "an adaptive network model," "a
multiplex model." Substrate is the least informative of the four axes: it says nothing
about where the behavior comes from. The survey is therefore organized by **generator**
— what produces the behavioral action — because that is the axis on which a new
framework has to differentiate itself.

## Caveat

Citation details were compiled from web search and should be verified against publisher
records before use in a manuscript. Year, volume and page numbers in particular.
