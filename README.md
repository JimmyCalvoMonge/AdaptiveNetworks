# Adaptive algorithms for human behavior in networks epidemic models

:cactus:

*Abstract*

We couple an epidemic model of disease transmission on networks and a human behavior framework to explicitly model behavioral decisions driven by risk-benefits trade-offs during epidemics. 
We let the population-level epidemic and behavior dynamics emerge from individuals' behavioral responses, modifying their neighborhood structure by temporarily dropping off or maintaining neighbor's connections. Our results suggest that behavioral responses induced by localized perceptions produce an early behavioral response at the population scale relative to the epidemic dynamics. Specifically, we show that susceptible individuals' behavioral responses are maximal at the epidemic peak. However, the population-level behavioral effort is relatively small at the epidemic peak. Our implementation is robust to different risk aversion distributions in the population, thus providing a framework to consider heterogeneous sub-populations in network epidemic models, each with different individual-level responses towards the disease.
